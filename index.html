02, The story

The comeback

One comeback season to a National A win

Nicola Quaye is a 20-year-old Manx rider who had natural talent as a junior but gradually fell out of love with the sport. At the end of sixth form she took a full-time job as a nail technician and stepped away from racing.

Last August she got the itch again, went out for one ride, and got hooked. She started training that autumn, completely self-coached, adapting everything to fit herself, driven by the data and her own improvement.

Less than a year later she soloed away on the first lap of the 75-mile Curlew Cup and stayed away for a remarkable maiden National A win, taking the Queen of the Ryals climbing prize for good measure, the kind of front-foot, all-in ride the track is made for.

04, Why the track, why now

Built for the boards

The Curlew Cup showed exactly what the track rewards: a huge engine, front-foot aggression, and the nerve to commit early and hold on. Now Nicola is going to hit the track hard.

Endurance track racing, the points race, scratch, elimination and madison, is made for a rider who attacks, reads a race and can suffer at the front. It is also where a single season of focused work turns raw ability into results fast.

And there is heritage. The Isle of Man is one of British cycling's great track nurseries, a small island that keeps producing world-class track riders. Nicola is the next Manx rider heading for the boards.
